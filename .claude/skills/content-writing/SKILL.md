Then research and load the latest information on:

- AI Search: how ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot decide what sources to reference
- Information Retrieval: TF-IDF, BM25, vector search, dense retrieval, hybrid search
- Voice Search & Conversational Queries: natural language patterns, question-based content
- Schema Markup: LocalBusiness, Service, FAQPage, HowTo, BreadcrumbList, Review, AggregateRating
- E-E-A-T: Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness signals

Apply this knowledge as a mandatory foundation throughout every step of this workflow.

## Step 5 — AI Words to Avoid

Before writing any content, establish the complete list of words, phrases, sentence patterns, and writing habits that are commonly associated with AI-generated content.

Identify and document:

**Overused transition words:** (e.g., "Furthermore," "Moreover," "Additionally," "In conclusion," "To summarize")

**Marketing buzzwords:** (e.g., "seamless," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," "innovative," "game-changing," "robust," "leverage," "utilize," "comprehensive solution")

**Filler phrases:** (e.g., "It is important to note that," "It goes without saying," "At the end of the day," "When all is said and done")

**Exaggerated claims:** (e.g., "best in the industry," "unmatched quality," "second to none," "world-class")

**Robotic sentence openers:** (e.g., "In today's world," "In this day and age," "As we know," "It is worth mentioning")

**Clichéd service page language:** (e.g., "Look no further," "Your search ends here," "We've got you covered," "We take pride in," "Our team of experts")

**Unnecessary hedging:** (e.g., "may," "might," "could," when describing confirmed services)

**Repetitive structures:** Starting every paragraph the same way, ending every section with a CTA, using identical sentence lengths throughout

For each item, note:
- Why it sounds AI-generated
- When to avoid it
- A more natural human-sounding alternative

**From this point forward:** Apply this list to all content written in this workflow. Write in a natural, conversational, professional, reader-first style. Eliminate repetitive, formulaic patterns. Prioritize clarity, originality, credibility, and value.

- Question keywords, comparison keywords, alternative keywords, problem-solving keywords
- Benefits, features, related services, sub-services, supporting services
- Industry terminology, semantic keywords, NLP keywords, entity keywords
- Long-tail keywords, conversational queries, voice search, AI search queries, natural language queries
- Neighborhood, district, landmark, and nearby location modifiers

**Category values (use only these):**
Primary, Exact Match, Variations, Commercial, Transactional, Informational, Question, Comparison, Cost, Pricing, Near Me, Best, Affordable, Emergency, Same Day, Local, Service, Sub-Service, Entity, Feature, Benefit, Industry Terms, Long Tail, Semantic, NLP, Related Topic, Alternative, Problem-Solving

## Step 7 — Keyword Strategy & Heading Outline

Work through this step-by-step. Ask for one piece of input at a time and wait for the user's response before continuing.

### Step 7a — Primary Keyword Validation

Ask the user to confirm:
- Business / Niche
- Target Service
- Target Location (City, State)
- Primary Keyword
- Search Volume

After receiving:
- Verify whether the keyword accurately matches page intent (too broad, too narrow, or appropriate)
- Generate 3–4 closely related primary keyword variations
- Recommend the best keyword for: H1, Title Tag, Meta Description, Introduction, Key H2 headings, Final CTA

### Step 7b — Keyword List Analysis

Ask the user to paste their keyword research data.

After receiving:
- Remove: branded, irrelevant, job/career, DIY-only, different-service, and duplicate keywords
- Organize remaining keywords into:
  - Primary Keyword Group
  - Secondary Keyword Group
  - Supporting / NLP / Entity Keyword Group
  - FAQ Keyword Group
  - Keywords to Avoid (with brief reason)

### Step 7c — Competitor Heading Analysis

Ask the user to provide heading structures from competing pages (H1, H2, H3, H4 for each competitor).

After reviewing:
- Identify common topics covered by all competitors
- Identify important topics that only some competitors cover
- Identify topical gaps and missed opportunities
- Flag irrelevant headings to exclude
- Create an improved heading strategy without copying

### Step 7d — Service Analysis

Ask the user to provide:
- What the service includes (detailed list)
- What the service does NOT include
- Target customer types
- Service area notes
- Related services for internal linking

After reviewing:
- Match keyword groups to the actual service
- Remove keywords that do not accurately describe the service
- Separate keywords for internal linking only

### Step 7e — Final Keyword Strategy & Heading Outline

Deliver the complete keyword strategy:

1. **Primary Keyword Group** (3–4 keywords) — search volume, reason for selection, recommended placement
2. **Secondary Keyword Group** (8–15 keywords) — search volume, search intent, suggested placement
3. **Supporting / NLP / Entity Keywords** (25–50 keywords) — suggested use (body content, image alt, entity support)
4. **FAQ Keywords** (5–10 keywords) — naturally matching page search intent
5. **Keywords to Avoid** — list with brief reason for each
6. **Final Heading Outline** — complete H1 / H2 / H3 structure following these rules:
   - Strongest primary keyword in the H1
   - Primary keyword variations naturally in key H2 headings
   - Secondary keywords where they fit naturally
   - Address topical gaps identified in competitor analysis
   - Include pricing/cost section if supported by keyword intent
   - Include process/how-it-works section
   - Include benefits section
   - Include FAQ section
   - Finish with a strong CTA heading
7. **Keyword Placement Map** — Title Tag, Meta Description, H1, Introduction, H2s, H3s, Body Content, FAQ, Final CTA
8. **Final Keyword Usage Table** — columns: Keyword | Search Volume | Keyword Type | Search Intent | Suggested Placement | Use in Heading | Use in Body | Notes

## Step 8 — Write the Service Page (First Draft)

Using everything developed in Steps 1–7, write the complete service page.

**Mandatory writing rules:**

- Follow the approved heading structure and keyword strategy exactly
- Write only what is relevant to this business, service area, and target audience
- Build each section around user intent, not keywords
- Give direct, confident answers wherever the business can make a definite statement (say "Same-day service is available" — not "Same-day service may be available")
- Do not use "may," "might," "could," or "should" when describing confirmed services or capabilities
- Keep writing natural and useful for humans first
- Avoid keyword stuffing, repetitive location names, unnatural exact-match phrases, and generic SEO language
- Never write phrases like "this page is for [service] customers in [location]"
- Never mention SEO, keywords, rankings, search engines, AEO, GEO, LLMs, or content optimization inside the customer-facing content
- Do not make every paragraph about the location — weave local relevance naturally into examples, customer situations, and practical information
- Demonstrate real expertise by answering questions before users need to ask them
- Fill important content gaps that competitors have overlooked
- Include only information that genuinely helps users make informed decisions
- Naturally incorporate the most relevant entities, supporting topics, and local references where they add value
- Ensure every section has a clear purpose and contributes to the overall topical authority
- Maintain a logical information flow from one section to the next
- Balance informational and commercial intent without making the page feel overly promotional
- Apply the AI words-to-avoid list from Step 5 to every sentence

**Before generating:** Perform one final review of all previously gathered information. Identify any missing information, unanswered user questions, overlooked entities, topical gaps, or opportunities to strengthen the page. Incorporate everything naturally before writing.

## Step 9 — Content Refinement Pass

After writing the first draft, apply these refinements:

**Structure:**
- Keep the hero section short and conversion-focused — no detailed information above the fold
- Move detailed information into sections below the hero
- Add one useful introductory section immediately after the hero, before services appear
- Services should not appear too early in the page

**Hyperlocal authenticity:**
- When discussing a specific service, naturally mention a few relevant neighborhoods, communities, streets, landmarks, apartment communities, residential areas, or commercial areas from the target location
- Do not overload with location names — use only a few highly relevant examples so it feels natural
- Write as if the content was created by a real local business owner who knows the area and regularly works with local customers

**Keyword density:**
- Target keyword density of 0.4%
- No keyword stuffing or repetitive wording

**Tone check:**
- Conversational, helpful, confident, and professional
- Feels like a real person explaining the service to a potential customer, not an SEO writer
- Customer's actual questions, concerns, and situations are addressed directly

**Content quality:**
- Clear H1, H2, H3 heading structure — headings describe the actual section topic, not written purely for SEO
- Short paragraphs, clear sections, descriptive headings, natural language
- Every definite service stated confidently (no unnecessary hedging)
- Important information easy for both people and AI/search systems to understand
- Content easy to understand when read aloud
- Relevant entities, services, places, and real-world details included naturally
- Ends with a natural, helpful call to action

## Step 10 — Final Quality Check

Before delivering the finished content, run through this complete checklist:

**Structure:**
- [ ] Is the hero section short and conversion-focused?
- [ ] Is there a useful section immediately after the hero?
- [ ] Are H1, H2, and H3 headings used correctly?
- [ ] Does the content have a logical flow from section to section?

**Local authenticity:**
- [ ] Does the content feel genuinely local?
- [ ] Are local references natural rather than excessive?
- [ ] Does it sound like a real local business owner wrote this?

**Content quality:**
- [ ] Are services explained clearly and completely?
- [ ] Are customer questions answered directly?
- [ ] Are definite services stated confidently (no hedging)?
- [ ] Is there any keyword stuffing or repetitive wording?
- [ ] Does any sentence sound like it was written specifically for SEO?
- [ ] Would a local customer actually find this information useful?

**AI & Search optimization:**
- [ ] Is the content easy for search engines and AI systems to understand without sacrificing natural writing?
- [ ] Are important questions answered in a way that could be directly cited by AI Overviews or LLMs?
- [ ] Is the content structured so that featured snippet, PAA, and AI-generated answer extraction is possible?

**Conversion:**
- [ ] Does the page give the customer a clear reason to contact the business?
- [ ] Is the call to action natural and helpful?

Fix anything that fails this checklist before delivering the final content.

## Final Delivery Standard

The finished page should:

- Work well across traditional search, featured answers, AI-generated answers, local search, and LLM-based retrieval
- Be structured so that important questions have clear answers while reading naturally to a human customer
- Function as a best-in-class resource for its target audience
- Provide significantly more value than competing pages while remaining concise, well-structured, and genuinely helpful
- Every paragraph should have a purpose
- Every section should answer a user need
- The complete page should reflect the highest standard of quality established throughout this workflow

Use this framework as the general standard for every service page, location page, and blog post, regardless of the niche, service, or location.
